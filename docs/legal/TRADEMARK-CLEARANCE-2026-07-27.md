# SightAdapt preliminary trademark clearance decision

**Review date:** 2026-07-27  
**Reviewed project version:** 0.5.0.50-alpha  
**Issue:** #88  
**Status:** preliminary public-source review; not a professional legal clearance opinion

## Decision

The `SightAdapt` name must **not** be described as legally cleared and should not be filed, promoted through a major application store, or used for significant paid marketing until a qualified trademark professional completes an official-register clearance review.

The immediate risk is not an exact public use of `SightAdapt`. The material risk is the established use of the highly similar name `SightAid` by several visual-assistance products and projects serving the same or closely related users.

**Preliminary risk rating: medium-high.**

Recommended action before a commercial or store launch: prepare and clear a replacement name rather than investing further in `SightAdapt` branding.

## Intended territories and channels

Current intended use considered by this review:

- active development and official marketing in Poland;
- direct distribution and marketing throughout the European Union;
- global passive availability through a public GitHub repository and GitHub release artifacts;
- possible future publication through Microsoft Store or another major application store;
- possible future commercial licensing or enterprise distribution.

A professional review must expand the territory list before active marketing outside Poland and the EU.

## Product and relevant goods/services

SightAdapt is downloadable Windows software that applies configurable visual transformations to selected application windows and native popup menus as an accessibility/personalization function.

Preliminary Nice classes for professional confirmation:

- **Class 9 — primary:** downloadable computer software; downloadable accessibility software; downloadable display and color-adjustment software.
- **Class 42 — conditional:** software development, hosted software, SaaS or technical software services, only if such services will actually be offered under the mark.
- **Class 44 — avoid without regulatory review:** medical or health services are not part of the currently approved intended purpose.

The goods/services specification should remain narrow and accurate. The final wording and classes must be approved by a trademark professional.

## Exact-name result

No independent third-party application, company or commercial product using the exact combined spelling `SightAdapt` was identified in the reviewed indexed sources.

This is weak positive evidence only. It does not establish availability because:

- not every registered mark is reliably indexed by general search engines;
- applications can be pending, unpublished, recently filed or present only in a national register;
- unregistered earlier rights and business names can also be relevant;
- similarity, not only identity, can create a conflict.

## Material similar-name findings

### 1. SightAid — Google Play visual-assistance application

A Google Play application named `SightAid`, published by Ken Itakura, is marketed for people with visual impairments and uses voice/AI assistance.

Source: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nlpaas.sightaid

Risk relevance:

- same `Sight` prefix;
- short second element beginning with `A`;
- same accessibility and low-vision market;
- both are software applications;
- likely overlap in store searches, referrals and spoken recommendations.

### 2. Blind Assistant: SightAid — Apple App Store

An Apple App Store application named `Blind Assistant: SightAid`, published by Irfan Aksu, assists blind and low-vision users through sound, speech and haptic feedback.

Source: https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/blind-assistant-sightaid/id6766604709

Risk relevance: direct overlap in software, accessibility purpose and target users.

### 3. SightAid — Google Gemini developer competition project

A project named `SightAid` was published through the Google Gemini API Developer Competition as an application supporting visually impaired users with voice recognition and OCR.

Source: https://ai.google.dev/competition/projects/sightaid

### 4. SightAid — wearable visual-assistance research system

A peer-reviewed 2024 publication uses `SightAid` for a deep-learning wearable vision system for visually impaired users.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00521-024-09619-9

This demonstrates prior public use of the term in the same technology and accessibility field even if it does not by itself establish trademark rights.

### 5. SightAid — additional accessibility and low-vision projects

Additional public projects use `SightAid` or `Sight-Aid` for visual assistance, object recognition, medication/label support or accessible interfaces.

Examples:

- https://builder.aws.com/content/3ASUkjJP7ZrqcIdlG9fYrxTq6eF/sightaid-how-ai-helps-visually-impaired-individuals-safely-self-administer-medications
- https://www.nasyavaz.com/sight-aid

The repeated third-party adoption indicates a crowded naming field and weak exclusivity around `Sight` plus an assistance/adaptation term.

## Similarity assessment

### Visual

`SightAdapt` and `SightAid` are not visually identical. They share the complete first element `Sight` and begin the second element with `A`. The length difference reduces but does not remove similarity.

### Phonetic

Both names begin with the identical spoken word `sight`. In ordinary speech the distinctive attention is likely to fall on that first element. `Adapt` and `Aid` differ, but both communicate help or modification for vision.

### Conceptual

The concepts are close:

- `SightAid` communicates assistance with sight.
- `SightAdapt` communicates adapting sight or adapting an interface for sight.

Both are used for software directed at visual accessibility. Conceptual proximity is therefore high.

### Goods, channels and users

The overlap is substantial:

- downloadable software/applications;
- visual-accessibility or low-vision users;
- application stores and web search;
- assistive-technology recommendations;
- overlapping professional and consumer audiences.

### Overall

The exact names are distinguishable, but the shared weak/descriptive naming structure and market overlap create a meaningful risk of association or confusion. The existence of several `SightAid` projects also makes it difficult to build strong exclusive rights in another `Sight + assistance` name.

## Distinctiveness assessment

`Sight` directly refers to vision. `Adapt` suggests adaptation or configurable accessibility. For software that adapts visual presentation, the combined name is suggestive and may be considered partly descriptive.

This creates two independent commercial problems:

1. potentially narrower enforceable protection;
2. a greater likelihood that similar names already coexist in the market.

A more arbitrary or coined mark would provide a stronger registration and enforcement position.

## Logo review

The current tray icon is generated by project code and consists of:

- a rounded dark square;
- a white eye outline/form;
- a colored iris and pupil;
- a small colored notch/state marker.

The implementation is in `src/SightAdapt/TrayIconSet.cs`.

Eye symbols are common in visual-accessibility, vision, monitoring and image-processing software. A notable nearby example is Microsoft's Seeing AI branding, which also uses a white stylized eye on a colored rounded-square field.

The current icon is not an apparent copy: its dark gradient, filled eye, circular multicolor iris, pupil, highlight and state marker are materially different. Nevertheless, the basic eye-on-rounded-square concept is weak and crowded. It should not be relied upon as the principal source of distinctiveness.

Preliminary logo risk:

- copyright-copying risk: low based on the code-generated geometry reviewed;
- trademark/confusion risk: low-to-medium in isolation;
- registration strength: weak-to-medium because the eye concept is common in the field.

A replacement brand should use a more distinctive symbol and should receive a separate image-similarity search.

## Registration decision

### Current mark

Do **not** file `SightAdapt` as a word mark or logo mark until a professional reviews official registers and the similar `SightAid` uses.

Given the current findings, the preferred commercial decision is to rename before:

- Microsoft Store or other major-store publication;
- paid marketing;
- enterprise licensing;
- trademark filing;
- significant investment in branded domains, design or printed materials.

### Word mark versus logo

For a replacement brand, seek professional advice on filing:

1. the word mark first, because it protects use independent of visual styling;
2. a separate logo mark only after the final design is stable and visually cleared.

Do not rely on a combined word-and-logo registration as a substitute for word-mark protection.

## Fallback naming brief

Working candidates for a new naming round:

- `PanePrism`
- `ChromaRelay`

These are **not cleared names**. A limited ordinary web search did not reveal an obvious exact software conflict, but each candidate requires the same official-register, company-name, domain, store and visual review before adoption.

Naming requirements:

- avoid `Sight`, `Vision`, `Eye`, `See`, `Aid`, `Assist` and direct medical terminology;
- prefer a coined or arbitrary expression;
- remain pronounceable in Polish and English;
- avoid describing color correction or disability directly;
- secure matching repository, domain and store identifiers before announcement;
- perform knockout searches before design work and professional full searches before launch.

## Migration plan if the current name is rejected

1. Select three to five coined candidates using the naming brief.
2. Perform same-day web, store, repository, company and domain knockout searches.
3. Submit the surviving candidates for professional EU/Poland trademark searches.
4. Reserve domains, source-hosting organization/repository names and store identifiers only after preliminary approval.
5. Select and separately clear a distinctive logo.
6. Update assembly metadata, executable name, settings directory and documentation through a versioned migration.
7. Preserve compatibility with `%LOCALAPPDATA%\SightAdapt\settings.json` by migrating or reading the legacy location.
8. Add a temporary `formerly SightAdapt` notice only for the transition period.
9. Avoid redirect or metadata wording that implies ownership of unrelated `SightAid` products.
10. File the approved word mark before substantial public launch where commercially justified.

## Required professional work

The professional should receive:

The opinion should cover exact and similar word marks, unregistered rights where reasonably searchable, business names and visual-mark similarity.

## Safe public statement

Until professional clearance is obtained, the project may state:

> A preliminary public-source brand review identified no exact indexed use of `SightAdapt`, but found several similar `SightAid` visual-accessibility products. The current name is not legally cleared and is under review.

The project must not state that the mark is available, registered, protected, conflict-free or non-infringing.

## Conclusion

The preliminary result is **not approval of the current brand**. Because a close name is already used repeatedly in the same accessibility field, continuing with `SightAdapt` creates avoidable brand and legal uncertainty. The economically safer path is to clear a more distinctive replacement name before major distribution or commercial investment.
